Medtronic Ablation · Israel03
Portfolio

Two modalities, three platforms

Microwave ablation for soft tissue; radiofrequency for bone and selected liver work.

Microwave · Flagship

Emprint™ HP

150 W, 2.45 GHz generator with patented Thermosphere™ technology — thermal + field + wavelength control for a predictable, spherical zone. Single antenna (13 G, 15/20/30 cm).

Microwave · Legacy

Emprint™ 100 W

The original generator, still the installed base at many Israeli sites. Same antenna ecosystem; lower power and volume than HP.

Radiofrequency

Cool-tip™ RF E-Series

Touch-screen RF generator with chilled-saline electrodes (single / cluster / multiple). The de-facto bone & osteoid-osteoma tool in this market; also non-resectable liver.

Clinical headline (Emprint HP): up to 40% more volume with one antenna · sphericity 0.97 · ablative margin >5 mm · 3.57% local tumor progression at 12 months · 93% tumor control at one month.

Field structure

The business runs as two franchises

Different specialists, different systems, different buying behaviour.

🫀 Liver & kidney — Microwave

Driven by HPB surgeons (in OR) and interventional radiologists (under CT / ultrasound).

  • Platforms: Emprint HP / 100 W
  • Surgeons prefer resection; ablate selectively. IRs drive percutaneous volume.
  • Anchor accounts: Hadassah, Ichilov, Rambam, Sheba, Carmel, Beilinson.

🦴 Bone / osteoid osteoma — RF

Driven by orthopedic-oncology and skeletal radiology.

  • Platform: Cool-tip RF E-Series
  • Often requires rep presence at every case.
  • Centres: Rambam, Shaare Zedek, Ichilov (>30 cases/yr), HaEmek, Novomed/Maxed.

Patterns & risks

What the field map is telling us

🚚 System shuttling

~8 sites have no permanent generator — units are physically transported to each procedure, often with the rep present. High logistics & time cost.

🧍 Rep dependency

Several sites require rep attendance at every case (Rambam & HaEmek bone, Shaare Zedek IR, Hadassah gatekeeping). Caps how much the territory can scale.

❄️ Cryo erosion

Beilinson and Laniado shifting cases to cryoablation — driven by anesthesiologist shortage, not by a product gap we can spec-sheet our way out of.

📄 Contract & data gaps

Assaf Harofeh runs a system with no signed agreement; the account map has missing phones, emails and roles in many rows.

Fleet governance

The fleet is real revenue — poorly governed

  • Unresolved ownership rule: for each fixed-asset unit it is still undecided whether the customer pays for the PM check or returns the device.
  • Lapsed agreements: Beilinson (Clalit) did not renew; Carmel and Soroka contracts lapsed and had to be re-issued for 2026.
  • Quality/safety flag: a lab unit has a known defect (buttons can detach during use) and remains unrepaired due to the registration block.
  • Inconsistent billing: "customer pays for one check, the other is free" — discretionary calls instead of a standard rule.

Recommendations

How to improve the process

Four moves the data points to directly.

1

Place systems where volume justifies it

Convert recurring "shuttle" sites to consignment / placed units. Cuts transport cost, frees rep time, locks out competitors.

2

Build physician independence

Structured training & certification so high-need sites (bone teams, IR) run cases without a rep — scaling capacity instead of attendance.

3

Govern the capital fleet

Reconcile SAP locations, recover lost/faulty units, set one "pay-or-return" rule, auto-renew service contracts, close unbilled PM checks.

4

Answer the cryo threat

Lead with an anesthesia-light workflow story where MWA competes; defend Beilinson & Laniado before more cases shift.

Plus a quick win: close contract gaps (Assaf Harofeh) and clean the CRM data — missing contacts & roles are revenue waiting to be tracked.
